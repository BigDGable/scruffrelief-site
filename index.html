The short version

Why your beard itches, flakes and breaks out

Everyone's skin carries a yeast called Malassezia. Under a beard, warm skin and trapped oil give it plenty to eat. When it overgrows, skin reacts with flakes, itch, redness and small bumps.

Most beard oils are made from plant oils that feed it. Scruff Relief is Malassezia-safe, which means nothing in it feeds that yeast.

Beard dandruff

White flakes on dark shirts and an itchy chin. It's a mild form of seborrheic dermatitis, linked to too much Malassezia.

Beard itch

Constant itch under the beard, often with red, greasy-looking skin. Seborrheic dermatitis is a common cause.

Beard acne

Small, red, itchy bumps along the beard line. When yeast is behind them it's called fungal acne, and regular acne products do nothing for it.

Red, scaly patches

Redness or discolored patches with light scaling. Seborrheic dermatitis and tinea versicolor both involve the same yeast.

The science

It comes down to chain length.

Malassezia can't make its own fat, so it eats yours: the sebum your skin produces, plus any oil you put on it. Research shows it prefers fatty acids with carbon chains between 11 and 24 atoms long. Nearly every plant oil and butter used in beard care lands inside that range. Scruff Relief is built on squalane, a 30-carbon oil well outside it, and nothing else in our formula falls inside it.

Questions

Can beard oil cause acne?

Yes. Heavy oils like coconut can clog pores, and most plant oils feed the skin yeast behind fungal acne. Scruff Relief uses squalane, which is non-comedogenic and gives that yeast nothing to eat.

What causes beard dandruff and itch?

Usually an overgrowth of Malassezia, a yeast that lives on everyone's skin. Under a beard it feeds on your skin's oil and on most beard oils, and skin reacts with flakes and itch. Switching to an oil it can't eat takes away one of its food sources.

Will this cure my beard dandruff or seborrheic dermatitis?

No. Scruff Relief is a cosmetic beard oil, not a treatment. It's made so it won't feed the yeast involved, unlike most beard oils. If you have a diagnosed condition, keep working with your dermatologist.

What does Malassezia-safe mean?

Nothing in the product feeds Malassezia, the skin yeast linked to dandruff, seborrheic dermatitis and fungal acne.

Why not argan or jojoba? Every other brand uses them.

That is exactly the problem. Argan is mostly oleic acid (C18) and jojoba is mostly eicosenoic acid (C20). Both sit inside the range Malassezia feeds on.

What's in it besides squalane?

Squalane is the base. The rest is our own formulation, developed by our founder and screened so nothing in it feeds Malassezia. The full ingredient list is printed on every bottle.

Is it greasy?
