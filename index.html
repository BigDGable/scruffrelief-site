The short version

What does Malassezia-safe mean?

Malassezia is a yeast that lives on everyone's skin. In normal numbers it causes no trouble. When it overgrows, skin reacts with flaking, itch and redness.

A Malassezia-safe product contains nothing that feeds that yeast. Under a beard, where oil gets trapped against warm skin all day, that difference shows up fast.

Seborrheic dermatitis

Itchy, flaky, inflamed skin, thought to be the immune system reacting to too much Malassezia. Common on the scalp, face and under beards.

Dandruff

The milder version: flaking and itch without much inflammation. In a beard it shows up as white flakes on dark clothes.

Fungal acne

Small, red, itchy bumps from yeast in the hair follicles. Regular acne products do nothing for it.

Tinea versicolor

Discolored patches with mild itch and scaling, caused by the same yeast.

The science

It comes down to chain length.

Malassezia can't make its own fat, so it eats yours: the sebum your skin produces, plus any oil you put on it. Research shows it prefers fatty acids with carbon chains between 11 and 24 atoms long. Nearly every plant oil and butter used in beard care lands inside that range. Scruff Relief is built on squalane, a 30-carbon oil well outside it, and nothing else in our formula falls inside it.

Questions

Will this cure my seborrheic dermatitis?

No. Scruff Relief is a cosmetic beard oil, not a treatment. It is formulated without the oils that feed Malassezia, so it is designed not to make things worse. If you have a diagnosed condition, keep working with your dermatologist.

Why not argan or jojoba? Every other brand uses them.

That is exactly the problem. Argan is mostly oleic acid (C18) and jojoba is mostly eicosenoic acid (C20). Both sit inside the range Malassezia feeds on.

What's in it besides squalane?

Squalane is the base. The rest is our own formulation, developed by our founder and screened so nothing in it feeds Malassezia. The full ingredient list is printed on every bottle.

Is it greasy?
